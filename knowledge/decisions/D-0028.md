# D-0028 — The bag panel and the vanilla layout are laid out as one; the recipe book gets its room

Accepted 2026-09-25. Amends D-0027, whose "the recipe book, if open at such a width, may overlap
it; a narrow edge case" was wrong: photographed on the network fixture, the open book painted
over the panel at every width, since vanilla stands the book exactly where the panel was put,
and on screens narrower than 540 GUI pixels D-0027's shift of the screen after vanilla had laid
it out left the book's button 57 pixels left of the screen, over the player model.

## The rule

A worn bag makes the vanilla layout 180 pixels wider (the panel and its gap). The screen, and
the recipe book when it is open, are laid out as vanilla lays them out for a screen that much
wider, which moves them right by half of it, so panel and vanilla layout are centered as one;
vanilla builds its button from that position, so the button is where the screen is. Vanilla
stands the open book beside the screen from 379 wide up and over it below; with the panel the
same rule holds with the panel, its gap and the book's tab strip added (589), so the book stands
beside only where panel, tabs, book and screen all fit, and the panel then moves left of the
tabs. Where the open book cannot stand beside the panel, the book takes the room: the panel is
not shown, its cells are inactive, and vanilla lays the screen out as it would with no bag, so
the book's button stays where the player can reach it and closing the book brings the panel
back. Under 360 wide not even panel and screen fit, and the panel is never shown.

The rule is the pure `domain.InventoryPanel`, pinned by JUnit at 320, 359, 360, 370, 427 (a
friend's 1280 wide at GUI scale 3), 588, 589, 640 and 688 (Rusty's ultrawide at scale 5). The
screen mixin feeds vanilla the widened width at the three places it reads it (the book's own
layout, and the screen position at init and at the button's toggle), reads the book's open
state from the player's recipe book rather than the component (which copies it only when laid
out, so init saw a stale value in the first build), lays the book out again on a toggle that
changes the widening, and moves the forty cells to the panel through a mutable accessor on the
slot's x (vanilla fixes it at construction).

## What the photographs showed

The panel with the book closed at 640 and 427 wide, centered; the book beside the panel at 640
with its tabs clear of the panel by the gap; the panel yielding to the open book at 427 and 540
with vanilla's own layout and a reachable button; no panel at 320; the Curios button, which
sits inside the vanilla frame at the player box's corner, untouched throughout.

## Rejected

- The book between the panel and the screen (no slot moves needed): detaches the book from the
  inventory it belongs to.
- Overlaying the book on the screen wherever it cannot stand beside the panel, as vanilla does
  under 379: in vanilla's overlay mode the button is unreachable while the book is open, which
  would have stranded a bag-wearer at 427 wide with a book they could not close.
- Keeping the panel in the widened layout while the open book overlays (panel drawn, cells
  hidden): vanilla draws no slots in that mode, so the panel showed empty wells.

## EMI

EMI lays its sidebars out from the vanilla screen's rectangle and would paint its left one over
the panel. An EMI plugin, loaded only when EMI is installed, declares the panel's rectangle as
an exclusion area. EMI takes over the book's button (its default action toggles EMI's own
craftables), so on the pack the vanilla book never opens and the beside rule matters only to
players without EMI.
