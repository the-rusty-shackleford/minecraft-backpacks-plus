# D-0032 — Backpacks+ is a provider of the Carried protocol; pickups and lent ammo move into it

Accepted 2026-09-28. Rusty, after Village Deed could not see the emeralds in his bag: "we need to
make a single modification that makes all things that see your inventory also see your backpack.
This game of whack a mole is retarded." He approved the proposal: one protocol, Carried
(`minecraft-carried`, its D-0001), that answers inventory questions for a player with their bags
in it; Backpacks+ provides the bags; every consumer asks Carried. His calls on the proposal:
the name Carried; every carried bag counts, as D-0026 and D-0029 already had it, a second bag in
the chest slot too; the bugs the audit found fixed in the same change.

## What Backpacks+ provides

`BagStores` registers with `CarriedProviders` in the mod's constructor. For a player it lists,
in order: the bag they wear (the first Curios back slot holding one, else the chest slot, as
`BagLocations.worn` decides), any other bag on their back, then every bag in the inventory in its
order: the hotbar first, then the main slots, a bag in the chest slot while a back bag is worn
(new: D-0026 never looked there), then the offhand. Each is a `BagStore`: the bag's storage cells
and nothing else. **Mounts are never part of a store**: they are gear a player holstered, not
stock. That changes one thing players could see: D-0026 used to lend arrows out of a small mount
to a bow; it no longer does.

A store's cells are a copy of the bag's contents taken when the bag or its contents component
changed (the component is immutable, so a new instance is the only way the contents change and
comparing references is exact), so reading a cell costs nothing and nobody reading one can change
the bag. A write replaces the storage cells in one `BagContents.store` (one revision), the mounts
as they are; a bag open on screen is written through that screen's own container, cell by cell,
after every changed cell has been checked against the bag's rules, so the screen stays open
(D-0029's rule, kept).

## What moved into Carried

D-0029 (pickups: the inventory's stacks, the bags' stacks, an empty inventory slot, an empty bag
cell) and D-0026 (projectiles lent as a marked copy) are rules about carried storage, not about
backpacks, so they now live in Carried (its D-0002 and D-0003) and apply to any provider.
`BagPickup`, `BagAmmo`, their three mixins and `PickupPlan` (now Carried's `Placement`, with its
JUnit) are gone from here. Their GameTests stay here, run against real bags through Carried, and
pass unchanged (the ammo tests read `Carried.lent`).

D-0026's debit changed on the way, a bug the audit found: the bag paid for a lent arrow only when
the weapon went through vanilla's `useAmmo`. Create's potato cannon asks for a projectile the same
way, then shrinks the lent copy itself (bytecode: `ammo.shrink(1)` in `PotatoCannonItem.use`), so
it fired a bag's potatoes for nothing. Carried settles a loan by what the copy lost, at `useAmmo`
and again at the end of the tick (its D-0003); Carried's GameTest with a weapon that shrinks the
copy itself failed on the old rule (the store kept all 8 arrows after two shots) and passes on the
new one. Not reproduced with Create itself.

## Cost

HUDs count every frame and Ranged Weapons Mod's gunnery every tick, so listing a player's bags
allocates nothing once they have been seen: store objects are kept per player and source; the
inventory's lists are read directly, because `Inventory.getItem` walks its compartments with an
iterator (a first version allocated 1,344 bytes a count from that alone); with Curios the back
slots' handler is looked up once per game tick (the lookup allocates) and kept per player. Players
are keyed weakly and by identity: a client's player and the integrated server's are equal by
entity id and must not share stores. Measured by `ProviderGameTests.countingARealBagAllocatesNothing`
with the JVM's per-thread allocation counter: a count over a full inventory and a worn Expedition
bag takes 287 ns and allocates 0 bytes over 100,000 calls; with Curios loaded, 289 ns and 0 bytes.

## Found on the way: the gear sync failed other mods' GameTests

With Backpacks+ on their gametest servers (to prove them against real bags), Warehouse Manager's
suite failed six tests, most with no bag in them: "Payload backpacksplus:state may not be sent to
the client!". The gear sync announced a player's state to everyone tracking them through NeoForge's
send-to-trackers, which throws at the first connection that never negotiated the channel, a test's
mock beside them, and so fails the player's tick. In play every client has the channel (it is
required), but a mod that throws in another mod's tests is a mod nobody can test beside. It now
sends to each player watching the wearer's chunk only when their connection can take it (a
superset of the trackers; the client keeps a bounded table of states for players it has not
spawned, so a state sent a little wide is harmless). `SyncGameTests.aViewerWithoutTheChannelIsSkippedNotCrashed`
failed before and passes after. Quick Slot's own state broadcast has the same shape; its test here
declares Quick Slot's channel so only this mod's is missing, and Quick Slot is left for its own
change.

## Rejected

- Keeping the pickup and ammo code here and adding Carried beside it: a second kind of carried
  storage (a quiver) would then need its own copies, the whack-a-mole again.
- Listing a bag's mounts as a second, "gear" store: nothing asked for it, and every consumer would
  then have to know to leave it out.

## Verification

`ProviderGameTests` (5): storage counts and mounts never; every carried bag counted, worn first,
in the inventory's order; a take and a give through the bag's rules (portable storage refused);
a take from a bag open on screen keeps the screen open and shows the change; counting allocates
nothing. `CuriosGameTests.aSecondBagInTheChestSlotIsCarriedBehindTheBackBag`. All 63 GameTests of
0.5.1 pass unchanged on the provider; 73 in all, 81 with Curios. `AmmoGameTests` counted every
arrow in a wide box and cleared them, and tests in a batch run side by side closer than an arrow
flies: a neighbour's cleanup deleted its arrow once in the Curios run. It now counts and clears
only its own player's arrows; three Curios runs after the fix all green.
