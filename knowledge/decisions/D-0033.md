# D-0033 — The worn bag's cells at the crafting table; EMI counts and fills from them

Accepted 2026-09-28. Part of the Carried change (D-0032). The audit found that EMI never sees the
bag: its handlers take their "input sources" from fixed slot ranges (the inventory screen: grid
1–4 and inventory 9–44; the crafting table: 1–9 and 10–45, read in EMI 1.1.24's bytecode), so
neither D-0027's cells on the inventory screen nor anything at a table counted. Offered two ways
for the table, Rusty: "I kinda like b more": give the table the bag's panel, as chests have it
(D-0030), rather than (a), a handler that counts the bag and fills through vanilla's recipe-book
packet. (b) is also the one that works: the server's recipe placement refuses any recipe the
player has not unlocked (`ServerPlaceRecipe.recipeClicked` asks `ServerRecipeBook.contains`), so
(a) would have failed for most of what EMI shows.

## The table

- **Menu, both sides.** `CraftingMenuMixin` adds the forty cells after the table's 46 slots
  (vanilla's `CraftingMenu` only; a subclass is left alone), so they are real slots at the table.
  Registrar "5": the menu's slot layout changed, and a 0.5 client would be kicked at its first
  table.
- **Shift-click.** Vanilla's rules, plus one: what vanilla sends from the result or the grid to the
  inventory and cannot fit goes into the bag (storage before mounts), where vanilla dropped a
  result at the player's feet. From the inventory a stack goes to the grid as before; from a bag
  cell, to the inventory.
- **Screen.** `CraftingScreenMixin` lays the table out by the inventory screen's rule (D-0028,
  `InventoryPanel.of`), because the two screens are the same size and place their recipe book the
  same way: a worn bag widens the layout by the panel, the screen and an open book stand half a
  panel right, the book goes beside the screen only where panel, book and screen all fit and
  otherwise takes the room while the panel yields; under 360 wide no panel. The panel's top is
  level with the screen's, as on the inventory screen. Only the book button's place differs.

## EMI

`BagRecipeHandlers` are EMI's own inventory and crafting handlers with the bag's active storage
cells added to their input sources (never the mounts: gear, not ingredients). EMI then counts
craftables from the bag and its fill moves items out of the cells as out of any slot of the menu;
that works on a server without EMI (the pack's): the fixture's server had none, and EMI's fill
moved the bag's cobblestone into the table's grid through the cells' own rules. EMI uses the first handler for a menu that supports a recipe, and its
built-in plugin registers its own first, so the plugin puts these just ahead of EMI's own in its
list, behind any other mod's (`domain.HandlerOrder`). That list is in an EMI class outside its API
(`EmiRecipeFiller.handlers`); if a later EMI moves it, the move is logged and the handlers stay
behind EMI's own, and the bag is simply not counted.

**Ahead of EMI's own, not at the front.** The first version put the table's handler at the front.
So does Warehouse Manager's (its D-0005), on purpose, so that EMI counts and fills from a managed
building. With both loaded, whichever registered last won: in the release gate's run of Warehouse
Manager's booth, this handler stood first, and EMI at a managed table stopped counting the building;
the other order would have hidden the bag at every table. Just ahead of EMI's own, the order is
Warehouse Manager's, this one, EMI's, whichever registers first, and Warehouse Manager's handler
takes its input slots from the one behind it (its D-0015), so EMI counts the building and the bag. The
plugin also keeps EMI's sidebars off the table's panel.

## Verification

`CraftingTableGameTests` (4): the table's menu carries the cells after its own and reads the worn
bag; a shift-clicked result goes into the bag when the inventory is full and nothing is dropped;
with room the result goes to the inventory and a bag cell shift-clicks into it; the recipe book
fills the table's grid from the bag (a stone pickaxe from sticks and cobblestone only in the bag).
JUnit `HandlerOrderTest` (8): the handler's place for every mix of EMI's own entries and another
mod's, and the same order whichever of two mods registers first. Warehouse Manager's booth, with
this jar and EMI on its client, asserts the three handlers' order at a managed table and that EMI
there counts and fills from a worn bag. On the fixture, photographed and driven with EMI on the client only, as in the pack
([record](../../devtools/verification/crafting-table.md)).
